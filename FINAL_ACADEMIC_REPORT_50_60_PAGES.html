ABSTRACT

The system implements a robust role-based access control architecture supporting four distinct user types: Students, Supervisors (designated as Staff), Administrators, and System Managers. Implementation utilizes the Laravel 12.0 PHP framework with a relational database architecture comprising 18 core tables, ensuring scalability, security, and data integrity. The system incorporates multiple payment gateway options including RabbitMaid for production deployments and a Simulator for testing environments.

  3. Dual-Sided Payment System: Student fee processing and staff compensation calculation based on supervision workload, with transparent component breakdown including base pay, fixed supervisor bonuses, and per-supervisee bonuses

CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION AND PROJECT OVERVIEW

1.1 Introduction

The management of internship programs represents one of the most critical yet challenging functions within educational institutions. In contemporary higher education, internship experiences serve not merely as supplementary learning opportunities but as fundamental components of professional development, bridging the gap between theoretical knowledge acquired in classrooms and practical competencies required in professional environments.

Traditional internship coordination involves numerous interconnected processes: student-supervisor assignment, task creation and grading, progress monitoring, payment processing, and communication coordination. These processes, when managed manually or through disconnected systems, consume significant institutional resources and remain highly error-prone. Manual coordination processes can consume hundreds of administrator hours annually, supervision quality becomes inconsistent, payment disputes arise from lack of transparency, academic progress tracking fragments across multiple documents and systems, and institutional compliance and audit trails become difficult to maintain.

The Internship Management System emerges as a comprehensive solution to address these multifaceted complexities. Unlike typical e-learning systems designed as supplementary tools, this project envisions a complete, fully integrated platform where internship management, payment processing, supervision coordination, academic task tracking, and administrative operations converge seamlessly into a unified digital ecosystem.

1.2 Background and Problem Statement

Educational institutions face significant challenges in managing internship programs efficiently and transparently:

1.2.1 Specific Problems Identified

  • Supervision Fragmentation: Supervision requests, transfers, and revocations are tracked manually across different systems or paper documents, creating multiple sources of truth, data inconsistencies, significant processing delays, and preventing real-time administrative oversight.
  • Payment Opacity: Staff and student payments lack transparent tracking and clear calculation methodologies. Payment disputes arise frequently, administrative overhead for dispute resolution consumes resources, and institutions face reputational risk from accusations of mismanagement.
  • Task Management Inefficiency: Creating, assigning, grading, and tracking tasks requires manual coordination. Multiple communication channels lead to missed deadlines, lost information, and inconsistent feedback quality.
  • Communication Delays: Important updates reach users slowly through email and informal channels. Critical messages are lost. Students miss deadlines due to inadequate notification mechanisms.
  • Limited Data Accessibility: Administrators cannot access real-time data on internship progress, completion rates, or student performance. Report generation requires manual data consolidation from multiple sources.
  • Scalability Limitations: Manual systems cannot efficiently scale as student numbers increase. Administrative burden grows linearly with student population, making expansion financially unsustainable.
  • Academic Integrity Concerns: Grading processes lack consistency and transparency. Evaluation frameworks are non-standardized, and recovery opportunities for students with poor grades are inequitably distributed.
  • Profile and Capacity Management: Students cannot easily complete required profile information before requesting supervision. Supervisors lack visibility into their capacity. Systems cannot enforce one-student-to-one-supervisor constraints reliably.

CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW AND THEORETICAL FRAMEWORKS

2.1 Theoretical Foundations of Educational Technology

2.1.1 E-Learning Systems Theory

Modern e-learning systems are built on principles that fundamentally transform the nature of educational delivery and coordination. The literature identifies several key principles essential for effective educational technology:

Accessibility Principle: Learning and management systems must be accessible to all users regardless of location, time zone, technical proficiency, or device type. This principle recognizes that diverse student populations have varying access patterns, schedules, and technical capabilities. Accessibility encompasses not only technical availability but also interface simplicity, support availability, and responsive design accommodating multiple device types.

Usability Principle: Interfaces must be intuitive and require minimal training. Complex, poorly-designed systems create barriers to adoption and generate excessive support overhead. Effective e-learning systems prioritize user experience, consistent navigation patterns, clear visual hierarchies, and contextual help systems.

Interactivity Principle: Systems should support active engagement rather than passive consumption. E-learning systems facilitate interaction between students and supervisors, among student cohorts, between students and academic content, and between users and the system itself. Interaction promotes deeper learning and engagement.

Scalability Principle: Solutions must handle growing user populations without degradation in performance, reliability, or user experience. Scalability encompasses database optimization, load balancing, caching strategies, and architectural design decisions that anticipate and support growth.

2.1.2 Business Process Optimization Theory

Business Process Management (BPM) theories inform the system design through several fundamental principles:

Process Automation: Repetitive tasks such as payment notifications, deadline reminders, and status updates should be automated to reduce human error and increase consistency. Automation frees human resources for activities requiring judgment and personal interaction.

Workflow Management: Complex processes involving multiple stakeholders and decision points require structured workflows that define clear stages, decision criteria, and role responsibilities. Supervision transfers exemplify such complex workflows requiring careful orchestration across multiple system components.

Error Reduction: Digital systems inherently reduce human errors in data entry, calculations, and information transfer. Structured data entry, automated calculations, and centralized information storage eliminate transcription errors and calculation mistakes common in manual systems.

Audit Trails: All transactions and decisions must be logged for compliance, dispute resolution, and accountability. Complete audit trails enable institutions to demonstrate that processes were followed correctly and provide evidence in cases of disputes or regulatory inquiries.

2.1.3 Financial Systems Theory

Payment processing adheres to fundamental principles of financial systems:

Transparency Principle: All transactions must be visible to relevant parties. Students must understand exactly what they owe and what they have paid. Supervisors must see how their compensation is calculated. Administrators must access complete payment histories. Transparency builds trust and reduces disputes.

Accuracy Principle: Calculations must be precise and verifiable. Financial systems cannot tolerate rounding errors, calculation mistakes, or inconsistent application of rules. Automated calculations with clearly documented formulas ensure consistency.

Security Principle: Financial data must be protected from unauthorized access and modification. Payment systems require authentication, authorization, encryption, and secure transaction processing.

Auditability Principle: All financial transactions must be traceable. The system must record who initiated each transaction, when it occurred, what amount was involved, what status it achieved, and who authorized it. Audit trails provide accountability and enable verification.

2.2 Conceptual Framework of the System

2.2.1 System Components Integration

The Internship Management System integrates five core, interdependent domains:

Academic Domain: Task creation, assignment, submission, and grading form the core academic function. Students complete assigned work, submit deliverables, receive grades with feedback, and track progress. Supervisors create appropriate tasks aligned with internship objectives, grade submissions fairly and consistently, and provide constructive feedback.

Supervision Domain: Supervision management encompasses request handling, assignment coordination, supervision transfers with audit logging, and revocation procedures. The system ensures each student has exactly one active supervisor at any given time, tracks supervisor capacity, and maintains complete history of all supervision changes.

Financial Domain: Payment accounts, payment settings, student payments, and staff payouts constitute the financial operations. The system processes student fees transparently, calculates staff compensation based on supervision workload, maintains transaction records, and integrates with external payment gateways.

Communication Domain: Internal messaging, notifications, announcements, and read-tracking enable effective communication. Students receive timely information about deadlines and grade postings. Supervisors receive notifications about new submissions. Administrators communicate policy changes to relevant user groups.

Administrative Domain: User management, system configuration, oversight dashboards, and reporting capabilities provide institutional control. Administrators create user accounts, assign roles, configure payment settings, monitor system health, and generate reports for institutional planning.

2.2.2 User Roles and Responsibilities

The system recognizes four distinct user roles, each with specific responsibilities and capabilities:

Student Role: Students represent the primary beneficiaries of the internship program. Their responsibilities include completing required profile information, requesting supervision from available supervisors, submitting assigned tasks by specified deadlines, viewing grades and feedback from supervisors, managing payment accounts and processing fees, communicating with supervisors regarding academic matters, and accessing progress dashboards to monitor their performance.

Supervisor Role (Staff Designation): Supervisors, designated as Staff in system roles, guide student learning and assess progress. Their responsibilities include completing profile and personal data requirements, submitting supervisor applications with capacity declarations, creating and assigning tasks to students, grading student submissions fairly and consistently, providing constructive feedback to students, managing their supervision capacity and student assignments, communicating directly with assigned students, and processing payment requests for their earned compensation.

Administrator Role: Administrators manage supervision coordination and operational oversight. Their responsibilities include approving or rejecting supervisor applications, assigning supervisors to students, processing supervision transfer requests with audit logging, managing user account lifecycle, monitoring payment processing, generating institutional reports, configuring system settings and policies, and resolving disputes and exceptions.

System Manager Role: System managers handle technical operations and institutional configuration. Their responsibilities include overall system administration, database management and backups, security and access control, system performance monitoring, integration with external systems, and advanced reporting and analytics.

2.3 Review of Related Systems and Literature

2.3.1 Existing Academic Management Systems

Several categories of systems currently serve educational institutions:

Learning Management Systems (LMS): Systems like Canvas, Blackboard, and Moodle provide course content management, assignment submission, and grading. However, they lack integrated supervision management, payment processing, or comprehensive internship-specific features. They are primarily designed for classroom-based courses rather than internship management.

Human Resources Management Systems (HRMS): Systems like Workday and ADP manage employee information, payroll, and benefits. While they excel at payment processing, they lack educational features like task management and grading. They are designed for employee management rather than student management.

Academic Administrative Systems (AAS): Systems like Banner and Colleague manage student registration, course scheduling, and academic records. They typically lack real-time collaboration features and detailed supervision management capabilities.

Gaps in Existing Solutions: Current market solutions lack: integrated supervision management specifically designed for internships, dual-sided payment processing supporting both student fees and staff compensation, real-time communication tailored to academic relationships, fine-grained task management with flexible grading, and architecture specifically optimized for developing countries' institutional contexts.

CHAPTER THREE: SYSTEM DESIGN AND ARCHITECTURE

3.1 System Architecture Overview

3.1.1 Three-Tier Architecture Model

The Internship Management System employs a classical three-tier architecture pattern, separating concerns into distinct layers to enhance maintainability, scalability, and testability:

Presentation Layer (Frontend): The presentation layer encompasses all user-facing interfaces and interactive components. This includes role-specific user interfaces tailored to each role (Student, Supervisor, Administrator), dashboard views providing role-specific information and action prompts, real-time notification displays alerting users to important events, form interfaces for data entry with comprehensive validation feedback, report generation interfaces for administrators, and responsive design ensuring functionality across devices (desktop, tablet, mobile). The presentation layer utilizes Tailwind CSS 4.0 for styling and Alpine.js for lightweight interactivity.

Application Layer (Backend): The application layer implements core business logic and system intelligence. This includes authentication and authorization services enforcing security policies, role-based access control middleware verifying user permissions, business logic for supervision workflows, task management, and payment processing, data validation and integrity checking before database operations, communication and notification services, integration points with external payment gateways, comprehensive logging and audit trail generation, and error handling with graceful failure management.

Data Layer (Database): The data layer provides persistent, reliable data storage through a relational database management system. This includes the relational database schema enforcing data integrity constraints, foreign key relationships maintaining referential consistency, uniqueness constraints preventing duplicate assignments, transaction management ensuring data consistency during complex multi-step operations, backup and recovery mechanisms for business continuity, and query optimization for performance at scale through appropriate indexing.

3.1.2 Technical Stack Components

Backend Framework: Laravel 12.0 provides the foundation for the backend implementation. This modern PHP framework offers robust routing capabilities, comprehensive middleware architecture for cross-cutting concerns, Eloquent ORM (Object-Relational Mapping) for database interactions, built-in security features including CSRF protection and password hashing, service container for dependency injection and loose coupling, and extensive ecosystem of packages extending functionality.

Frontend Technologies: Tailwind CSS 4.0 provides utility-first CSS framework enabling rapid UI development with consistent styling. Vite serves as the modern build tool providing fast development server and optimized production builds. Alpine.js offers lightweight JavaScript framework for interactive components without full SPA overhead. Blade Templating provides Laravel's native templating engine with clean, readable view files.

Database Options: The system supports multiple database backends. SQLite is used for development and small deployment environments due to simplicity and zero configuration requirements. PostgreSQL is recommended for production deployments requiring advanced features, superior scalability, and robustness. MySQL serves as an alternative production option.

Testing Framework: Pest PHP provides modern testing framework building on PHPUnit with elegant syntax and powerful assertions. Pest enables feature tests for complete user workflows and unit tests for business logic components.

3.3.1 Permission Hierarchy Implementation

Student Permissions: Students can view their own profile and personal data, request supervision from approved supervisors, view assigned supervisor information, submit assigned tasks before deadlines, view submitted task grades and feedback, view personal progress dashboard, create and manage payment accounts, view payment history and balance, send and receive messages with supervisors, and view announcements.

Supervisor (Staff) Permissions: Supervisors can complete and edit profile and personal data, apply to become supervisors, view supervisor application status, create and manage tasks for assigned students, view list of assigned students, grade task submissions with feedback, send and receive messages with students, view payment balance and history, request payment/payout, and view progress dashboard for assigned students.

Administrator Permissions: Admins can perform all student and supervisor functions for viewing purposes, create/read/update/delete user accounts, approve or reject supervisor applications, assign supervisors to students with capacity validation, transfer student supervision with audit logging, revoke supervision assignments, configure payment settings, process payments and payouts, view comprehensive system dashboards, generate reports, and send broadcast announcements.

System Manager Permissions: System managers have all administrator permissions plus database administration, system-wide configuration, performance monitoring, integration management, and audit log access.

3.3.2 Middleware and Policy-Based Authorization

Role-based access control is enforced through Laravel middleware checking user role against route requirements. The RoleMiddleware verifies user's role matches route requirements, allows admin bypass for system management purposes, and logs unauthorized access attempts. Model Policies encapsulate authorization logic for sensitive operations, preventing unauthorized modifications to data.

3.4 System Constraints and Business Rules

3.4.1 Critical Supervision Constraints

One-Student-to-One-Supervisor Constraint: At any given time, each student can have exactly one active supervisor. This critical constraint is enforced through: unique constraint on student_id in supervisor_assignments table (for active records), application logic checking for existing active assignment before creating new assignments, and automatic deactivation of previous assignment when new one is created.

Supervisor Capacity Constraint: Supervisors declare maximum number of students they can supervise. The system enforces this through: capacity check before approving supervision requests, capacity check before admin assignment, capacity check during supervision transfers, and real-time capacity calculation based on current active assignments.

Profile Completeness Requirement: Students must complete required profile information before requesting supervision through: system validation of required fields (department, phone, address), profile validation occurring at request time, and prevention of proceeding without completing required fields.

3.4.2 Task and Grading Constraints

Task Assignment Integrity: Individual tasks can only be assigned to students actively supervised by the supervisor. Group tasks can only be created by supervisors with at least one active supervisee. Special recovery tasks can only be assigned to students with prior negative grades.

Grading Permissions: Only the task supervisor can grade submissions. Supervisors can only grade submissions from their assigned students. Grading can use negative scores (minimum -1000) to identify deficiencies.

3.4.3 Payment Constraints

Account Requirement: Users must have an active payment account before payment processing. Account creation is a prerequisite to payment initiation.

Financial Calculations: Staff compensation is calculated as: base_pay + (supervisor_fixed_bonus if approved supervisor) + (per_supervisee_bonus × current_supervisee_count). All calculations are deterministic and auditable.

  • Payment records with status and external reference
  • Authentication attempts and access to sensitive data logged

4.1 Core Models and Relationships

4.1.1 User Management Models

User Model: The User model serves as the base for all users in the system. Key methods include: roleProfile() returning the appropriate role-specific profile (Admin, Staff, or Student), isAdmin()/isStaff()/isUser() role verification helpers, isOnline() checking if user was active in last 2 minutes for real-time status, updateLastSeen() updating last activity timestamp. Relationships include: sentMessages(), receivedMessages(), personalData(), paymentAccount(), and role-specific relationship accessors.

Admin Model: Extends user functionality with administrative features. Fields include appointed_date recording when admin was appointed, and future extensibility fields for departmental structure and organizational hierarchy.

Staff Model: Represents supervisors in the system. Fields include joined_date recording staff join date, linked to SupervisorApplication for supervisor application status, and relationships to supervision assignments and tasks created.

Student Model: Represents student users. Fields include enrollment_date recording student enrollment date, primary user type for internship participation, relationships to supervision requests, assignments, and task submissions.

4.1.2 Supervision Management Models

SupervisorApplication Model: Records supervisor application status. Attributes: staff_id, max_students (capacity declaration), status (pending/approved/rejected), admin_note (rejection reason). Relationships: staff() returns the supervising staff member. Business rules: only approved applications can be assigned to students, capacity limits enforced.

SupervisionRequest Model: Records student requests for supervision. Attributes: student_id, requested_supervisor_id, requested_admin_id, status, note. Relationships: student(), requestedSupervisor(), requestedAdmin(). Workflow states: pending → approved/rejected → completed or cancelled.

SupervisorAssignment Model: Records active supervision assignments (CRITICAL MODEL). Attributes: student_id (unique ensuring one-to-one), supervisor_id, assigned_by_admin_id, assigned_at, active. Relationships: student(), supervisor(), assignedBy(). Critical constraint: Unique constraint on student_id ensures one active supervisor per student.

SupervisionTransferLog Model: Maintains audit trail of supervision transfers. Attributes: student_id, from_supervisor_id, to_supervisor_id, performed_by_admin_id, transferred_at. Purpose: complete audit trail for compliance and dispute resolution. Relationships: Links to students, supervisors, and performing admin.

4.1.3 Academic Task Models

InternshipTask Model: Records academic tasks. Attributes: supervisor_id, assigned_student_id (nullable for group), title, description, due_at, max_score, active, is_special. Soft deletes implemented using SoftDeletes trait. Relationships: supervisor(), assignedStudent(), submissions(). Supports both individual and group tasks.

TaskSubmission Model: Records student submissions. Attributes: task_id, student_id, submitted_at, file_path (nullable), content (nullable), grade_score (nullable, negative allowed), graded_at, status. Supports both file uploads and text submissions. Allows negative grades for identifying deficiencies. Unique constraint on (task_id, student_id) preventing duplicate submissions.

4.1.4 Payment Models

PaymentAccount Model: Links users to external payment systems. Attributes: user_id, external_account_id. Prerequisite for payment processing. One-to-one relationship with users.

PaymentSetting Model: Configuration parameters. Attributes: key, value. Static methods: get() for retrieval with defaults, set() for updating/creating. Centralized configuration without hardcoding.

StudentPayment Model: Records student payment transactions. Attributes: student_id, amount_cents, currency, status, external_transfer_id, reference, note, paid_at. Relationships: student(). Tracks payment status and gateway reference ID.

StaffPayout Model: Records supervisor compensation. Attributes: staff_id, amount_cents, currency, supervisee_count, base_pay_cents, supervisor_fixed_bonus_cents, per_supervisee_bonus_cents, status, external_transfer_id, reference, note, paid_at. Relationships: staff(). Detailed breakdown of compensation components for transparency.

4.1.5 Communication Models

Message Model: Internal messaging system. Attributes: sender_id, receiver_id, recipient_type, subject, message, is_read, is_broadcast. Supports direct messages and broadcasts. Relationships: sender(), receiver(). Preserves message history for audit.

MessageUserRead Model: Tracks broadcast message reads. Attributes: message_id, user_id, read_at. Enables tracking which users have read announcements. Separate table for efficient broadcast tracking.

4.2.1 Student Supervision Controller

The StudentSupervisionController handles student supervision workflows. The create() method validates student has completed required profile information, checks student doesn't already have active supervisor, retrieves available supervisors with their capacity information, and returns view with supervisor options. The store() method validates profile completeness again, confirms student doesn't already have active supervisor, prevents duplicate pending requests to same supervisor, cancels previous pending requests (one pending at a time rule), and creates new SupervisionRequest in transaction.

4.2.2 Supervisor Task Controller

The SupervisorTaskController manages task creation and grading. The create() method retrieves students currently assigned to the supervisor and returns form for task creation. The store() method validates task type (normal or special) and assignment type (individual or group), for individual tasks verifies supervisor is actively supervising student, for special tasks verifies student has prior negative grade, for group tasks verifies supervisor has at least one active supervisee, and creates task record in database. The grade() method authorizes supervisor owns the task, for individual tasks verifies student assignment matches, for group tasks verifies student is supervised by supervisor, accepts grade score (including negative), status, and feedback, creates notification message to student, and records grade timestamp for audit trail.

4.2.3 Admin Supervision Management Controller

The AdminSupervisionManagementController handles supervision assignment and transfers. The store() method (assignment) validates student and supervisor existence and roles, checks supervisor is approved with sufficient capacity, in transaction deactivates previous assignment if exists and creates new active assignment, enforcing one-student-to-one-supervisor constraint. The transfer() method validates current assignment is active, validates new supervisor is approved with capacity, in transaction creates SupervisionTransferLog record, deactivates old assignment, creates new active assignment, and transfers individual tasks to new supervisor. The destroy() method (revocation) deactivates assignment marking supervision ended while preserving historical record.

PaymentGateway Interface: Defines contract for payment gateways. Methods include: createAccount(name, reference) creating new account in gateway, getBalance(externalAccountId) retrieving account balance, processPayment(...) initiating payment transaction.

StaffPaymentCalculator computes staff compensation. Takes supervisee count and supervisor status as input, calculates base pay component, calculates fixed supervisor bonus if approved, calculates per-supervisee bonus components, returns detailed breakdown with amount_cents field.

The ProfileController handles profile-related operations including profile creation and editing for all users, personal data viewing for users with supervisor applications, required information validation ensuring profile completeness, and redirect mechanism for incomplete profiles. Students and supervisors must complete profile information before certain actions: Students must complete profile before requesting supervision, Supervisors must complete personal data before applying to supervise. Profile includes department (required), phone (required), address (required), and bio (optional).

4.5 Database Migrations and Schema Management

Migrations are ordered by timestamp and follow logical grouping: core user and role tables, cache and job tables, communication tables (messages), supervision management tables, academic task tables, payment tables, and soft delete and special feature tables. System handles missing tables gracefully through application service provider checking for table existence, views conditionally rendering based on hasPersonalDataTable flag, and controllers validating table existence before accessing data. This resilience enables deployment in environments where migrations haven't been run yet.
